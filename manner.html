The Art of Good Manners

Welcome to the Manners section — a place to learn and practice good manners and etiquette. Start your day by reflecting on how you can show respect and kindness to others.



Everyday Manners:

Practice these simple manners every day to become more respectful, thoughtful, and kind.

  • "Every morning when you wake up say thank you Alhamdulillah / Thank God / Hari Ami Oor Prabhu Thank you for a beautiful day."
  • "Develop the habit of exchanging greetings with everyone in the family."
  • "Try to do your own work. Help with housework as much as possible."
  • "No matter how busy you are, set aside some time in the day for them. Your company will delight them."
  • "Before returning home greeting with parents. Chat with them, tell them what was interesting and interesting throughout the day and hear from them too."
  • "When going out, say where you're going and know if there's anything you need, bring, or want to eat something special."
  • "As people get older, they enter a second childhood. Don't be intolerant or resentful of their childish behavior. Be more tolerant and empathetic towards them."
  • "Don't cross your legs in front of olders/elders. Don't sit with your legs spread towards them."
  • "Stand up respectfully when elders come to speak."
  • "Take their dua/blessings before embarking on any good deed."
  • "Give priority to seniors waiting in any line."
  • "Always pray for the health and longevity of your parents."
  • "Before taking any big decision, let your parents know. Ask for their advice and prayers."
  • "Even if you're out, call at least once a day to check in."
  • "Do your best to keep them cheerful. Happiness of mind can wash away many of the ailments of their bodies in old age."
  • "Don't even think about sending them to an old age home if they feel like a burden/disagree. If you send them to an old age home, be prepared for the same fate in your old age."
  • "Remember with gratitude the contribution of your parents in your upbringing. And remember as a child - your heaven is under your mother's feet."
  • "Youth is the preparatory phase of life. It is the best time to make yourself capable and competent. Do not waste this time in idleness or indolence."
  • "Set a goal in life. Cultivate the belief that you are here on earth to do great things."
  • "Get into the habit of doing your own thing. You'll automatically make your own decisions."
  • "Start donating blood from your 19th birthday."
  • "Keep emotions under control in relationships with the opposite sex. Relationships will be beautiful."
  • "Spend leisure time in creative work and good people. Don't waste time in idle chats, roaming the streets and wandering in virtual worlds."
  • "Read good books for personal development, do regular meditation and quantum yoga."
  • "Participate in volunteering. Keep studying and work for the welfare of people with time, talent and effort. Only then can you do great things in life."
  • "Take care of yourself. Take time for yourself in the midst of your daily busyness."
  • "Don't hesitate to express your opinion. Don't suffer from inferiority complex. But also respect the opinion of others."
  • "Don't be radical about anything. Never be foolish enough to engage in controversy."
  • "Learn from the mistakes of others. Don't repeat the same mistakes in your life."
  • "Don't hold grudges against family elders. Remember, their prayers, good wishes and support will take you forward at every turn of life."
  • "Don't be inferior because of skin color or appearance. Respect and love yourself."
  • "Don't get angry by people's words. Remember, silence is the best answer."
  • "Be technological. Don't be a slave to technology."
  • "Self-centeredness hinders the development of intelligence and competence. So learn to get along with everyone."
  • "Avoid thinking that 'nothing will happen to me'. Say to yourself a hundred times every day - I believe, I am brave. I can, I will. I will build my life."
  • "Get out of the stubbornness of 'I'm bad'/'I'm just like that'. Remember, the person who can improve himself through constant effort is the one who is worthwhile as a human being."
  • "Don't be afraid of change. Face any situation with courage and conviction."
  • "Avoid using vulgar words, profanity, vulgar jokes or catchphrases in conversation while being so called smart. Such practices detract from personality."
  • "Avoid all intoxicants and drugs, including cold drinks and energy drinks."
  • "Make sure you know what you know about religion, politics, culture, etc. in any forum. If you're not sure, stay silent."
  • "Freedom does not mean arbitrariness or wantonness. Freedom means discipline and following rules. So see to it that you become disciplined. Only when you are disciplined can you enjoy freedom."
  • "Young age is the best time to meditate regularly. Regular meditation will make you humble yet courageous and confident."
  • "40 houses around your house (right-left-up-down-front and back) are neighbors. Get to know them. Keep in touch."
  • "Welcome any small gift from your neighbor."
  • "Stand as much as you can in the danger of your neighbor. If someone wants to harm him, resist as much as possible. Because if a fire breaks out in your neighbor's house, it can also destroy your house."
  • "If a neighbor dies, come forward with cremation/burial. Attend the funeral."
  • "Don't disturb the sleep or rest of the neighbors by playing loud music, watching TV or making noise during various events."
  • "Participate in your neighbor's joy. Build a bond of compassion with him. Again, keep the boundaries of relationship."
  • "If you cook something good, send even a little to your neighbor."
  • "Don't overburden the housekeeper/subordinate. Help him in his work as per your time, opportunity and ability."
  • "Prioritize her to-do list. Provide the maids with the best clothing you can."
  • "Though faithful, do not be dependent on him in every way. Do not keep the keys of the cupboard or valuables close to him."
  • "Let them eat what you eat. Give snacks/meals to part-time domestic workers too. Don't give leftovers and stale food."
  • "Never beat physically, do not abuse. Do not give trouble in living and eating. If you make a mistake, try to correct him by explaining; if not, let him go."
  • "Don't get angry or misbehave if they don't come to work on time or are absent."
  • "Say hello when you meet someone. Exchange greetings and compliments with a sincere smile."
  • "Avoid giving a clear answer or nodding your head even after hearing the greeting."
  • "If your hands are wet, sweaty or dirty, avoid shaking hands and smile and say, 'Sorry, wet hands.'"
  • "Dress neatly, elegantly and modestly."
  • "Don't come naked in front of others. Button your shirt properly."
  • "Don't make people feel uncomfortable by saying, 'This dress doesn't fit you.'"
  • "Take care not to disturb others by the strong smell of used perfume, the sound of shoe heels or ornaments."
  • "Abandon the slavish mentality of 'wearing the brand'. Prioritize the quality, utility, taste and modesty of clothing."
  • "Don't keep piles of clothes at home. Give the clothes you don't wear anymore to those who need them."
  • "Busy and successful people are punctual. So respect the time."
  • "Wake up early. Start the day with a smile on your face. The whole day will go well."
  • "Don't make excuses for not being punctual. It is possible to be punctual in any situation if you are aware."
  • "Leave a little earlier to avoid the traffic jam and reach the office/somewhere on time."
  • "Take the person who is punctual in the office or in the organization as an example. Don't get carried away because others are late."
  • "'I'm late because of traffic'—often such an excuse will undermine your position, negatively affecting your career advancement."
  • "Follow the rules and customs of the place."
  • "When the National Anthem is played, stand the entire time and show respect."
  • "Don't think that 'the rule is for everyone else, I'm the exception to the rule'. If you think you're above the rule, you'll sink into the quicksand of ego and self-destruction."

  • "Don't leave a companion behind. Don't fall too far behind yourself. Try to keep pace with your partner."
  • "Don't shout with joy when you see someone you know on the street. Talk about the situation. Smile when you make eye contact."
  • "Don't stand in a way that obstructs others' movement. Don't walk two or three people side by side/holding hands on the sidewalk/busy thoroughfare."
  • "If you are accompanied by a parent or elder, take their permission before taking any decision."
  • "Do not shuffle or make noise. Walk as quietly, submissively and humbly as possible."
  • "Avoid elbowing your way into the crowd."
  • "Express your sincere regret by saying 'sorry' when you are unexpectedly bumped or confronted."
  • "Don't talk on the phone while walking. Don't stare at the screen. Stand aside when it's an emergency call."
  • "Avoid talking on the phone, listening to music or the radio with headphones/earphones in your ears while crossing/walking the road."
  • "Remember the dead and pray for the dead people while passing by the cemetery/crematorium."
  • "Use the right hand when giving or taking something from someone."

  • "Ask permission before entering someone's room. Be polite and confident when entering a room."

  • "Never say that when someone is in trouble, 'I knew it would happen' or 'I told you so.'"
  • "Choose the virtues of others, avoid the faults."

  • "If you take something from someone, remember to return it in time."
  • "Think before you make a promise. If you make a promise, keep it. If you can't keep it, express regret."
  • "Be sincere and attentive when listening to someone."
  • "Speak slowly, clearly, and in a moderate tone."
  • "Be careful what you say to whom, where, and how much. Be good at speaking."
  • "Don't force your opinion and decision."
  • "Abandon the habit of exaggerating."
  • "Don't talk between two people. If you ever did, then say 'sorry.'"
  • "When listening to someone, pay full attention instead of looking around or looking at your mobile screen unnecessarily."
  • "Get up with permission if you need to get up in the middle of a conversation."
  • "If it's hard to understand or hear, say 'sorry, please say it again' instead of 'huh/yes/what'."
  • "Don't listen to rumors. Beware of those who spread rumours."
